## Project Overview

This project uses GIS to examine why Iceland has such an unusual concentration of volcanic, glacial, geothermal, and tectonic features.

Iceland is one of the few places where a major oceanic divergent plate boundary is exposed above sea level. I used spatial analysis to examine how volcano locations, glaciers, active volcanic zones, fault and fissure swarms, geothermal features, and selected case-study sites relate to one another across the island.

The project focuses on the relationship between these features and Iceland’s active volcanic and rift systems associated with the Mid-Atlantic Ridge.

## Research Question

**How are Iceland’s volcanic, glacial, and geothermal features spatially distributed, and how does this relate to active volcanic and rift zones associated with the Mid-Atlantic Ridge?**

## Objective

Use GIS to map and interpret the relationship between Iceland’s volcanoes, glaciers, geothermal areas, and active tectonic and volcanic structures.

## Methods

I added multiple spatial datasets to ArcGIS Pro, including:

- Glacier polygons
- Volcano point locations
- Iceland tectonic geology layers
- Active volcanic zones
- Fault swarms
- Fracture and fissure swarms
- Manually created case-study points

I then created a **25 km buffer around active volcanic zones**.

Using **Select by Location**, I identified volcano features that intersected this buffer and compared their distribution with Iceland’s active rift-volcanic structures.

## Results and Interpretation

The spatial analysis identified **38 volcano features intersecting the 25 km volcanic-zone buffer**.

This supports a strong spatial association between volcano locations and Iceland’s active rift-volcanic system.

Fracture and fissure swarms also form clear linear patterns across the landscape. These patterns help explain why volcanic and geothermal features are concentrated in particular areas rather than being randomly distributed.

Major glaciers overlap parts of this volcanic terrain, adding another layer of complexity to Iceland’s physical geography.

## How to Read the Map

The poster combines several geographic layers:

**Red/orange zones** represent active volcanic and rift zones.

**Purple lines** represent fracture and fissure swarms.

**Pale tan shading** represents the 25 km volcanic-zone buffer.

**Blue polygons** represent glaciers.

**Red triangles** represent volcanoes.

**Large symbols** identify selected case-study sites.

Together, these layers show how volcanic, tectonic, geothermal, and glacial systems overlap spatially.

## Conclusion

Iceland’s geologic uniqueness comes from the overlap of active rifting, hotspot volcanism, geothermal heat, and glaciation.

The GIS analysis shows that many of Iceland’s major volcanic and geothermal features are spatially connected to active volcanic and rift zones.

Glaciers add additional complexity because large ice masses overlap portions of the volcanic terrain, creating a landscape shaped by both intense geologic activity and glacial processes.
