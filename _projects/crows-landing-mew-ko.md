## Project Overview

This project compares Crows Landing with Mew-ko geographic descriptions recorded in the 1974 book *Boundary Descriptions of California Indian Stocks and Tribes* by C. Hart Merriam and Zenaida Merriam Talbot.

The map focuses on Tu-ol-um-ne and Yatch-a-chum-ne because their recorded descriptions are the most geographically relevant to Crows Landing. It uses rivers, named places, and directional descriptions from the historical source to examine Crows Landing’s geographic relationship to those descriptions.

This is a historical-source interpretation only. The project does not present an authoritative modern Tribal boundary, statement of sovereignty, land claim, proof of Tribal affiliation, or determination of cultural affiliation.

## My Role and Process

I developed this map by translating written geographic descriptions from *Boundary Descriptions of California Indian Stocks and Tribes* into a careful spatial comparison.

I identified the rivers, locations, and directional references named in the source, including the Stanislaus River, Tuolumne River, San Joaquin River, Knights Ferry, Stockton, and Mount Diablo.

I then used those features to construct an approximate interpretation of the Tu-ol-um-ne description and show the geographic relevance of the Yatch-a-chum-ne description to Crows Landing.

Because the source does not provide complete boundaries, I distinguished between source-supported features, approximate closures, and lines showing geographic proximity.

## Key Finding

Crows Landing is located south of the Tuolumne River and west of the San Joaquin River. On this map, it falls outside the approximate Tu-ol-um-ne area interpreted from the rivers named in the historical description.

The Yatch-a-chum-ne description is geographically relevant because the source places that group mainly west of the San Joaquin River. However, the source does not provide an exact southern boundary or state that the described range reached or included Crows Landing.

## Interpretive Limits

This map shows a geographic interpretation of a historical written source. It does not establish an exact historical boundary or provide evidence of modern Tribal affiliation.

Crows Landing was not used to construct the interpreted territory. It appears only as a reference point for spatial comparison.

## Source Used

C. Hart Merriam and Zenaida Merriam Talbot, *Boundary Descriptions of California Indian Stocks and Tribes* (1974), pages 14–15.
