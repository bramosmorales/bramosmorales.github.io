## Project Overview

I designed the commemorative plaque for the Smudging Space at Stanislaus State, an outdoor area near Village Lake dedicated to the traditional practice of smudging.

The project created a permanent marker for a campus space centered on ceremony, reflection, and cultural continuity. The dedication brought together campus representatives and members of local Native communities to recognize the space and its purpose.

## My Role and Process

I developed the plaque design and helped shape how the final piece would appear in a permanent outdoor setting.

My work included organizing the wording, refining the visual hierarchy, considering readability and placement, and assisting with the selection of the stone used for installation.

I worked closely with the NAGPRA/CalNAGPRA Coordinator throughout the project to ensure that the plaque was clear, respectful, visible, and connected to the purpose of the space.

## Design Considerations

The plaque needed to communicate clearly in an outdoor environment while remaining visually connected to the ceremonial and cultural purpose of the Smudging Space.

I considered how visitors would encounter the plaque, how the wording would be organized, how easily it could be read, and how the finished piece would relate to its stone base and surrounding landscape.

## Impact

The plaque gives the Smudging Space a permanent identity and helps visitors understand its purpose on campus.

It became part of a lasting outdoor space intended to support ceremony, reflection, recognition, and respectful engagement with Native communities.

The project also contributed to the public dedication of the space, connecting the design work to a larger campus and community event.
